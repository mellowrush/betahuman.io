The funny thing about expertise is that people start treating it like furniture.

Six years in the same industry and everyone assumes the shape is permanent.
You become the *React guy*, *the frontend guy*, *the safe pair of hands*. Before long, your career starts resembling a luxury hotel lobby: expensive, familiar and designed to discourage movement.

Then one day you notice you've become very efficient at solving problems you no longer find interesting.

That's when the trouble starts.

Most companies adore certainty. They hold meetings about it. They build roadmaps around it. Some promote people whose primary contribution is the confident repetition that stopped being true six months ago.

The version number on this site is **beta** because certainty has already had its turn.

Beta is not an apology. It's a refusal.

A refusal to pretend that expertise is a destination rather than a timestamp.

A refusual to publish only conclusions while hiding the years of confusion that produce them.

A refusal to wait until every idea has been approved by an imaginary committee that exists entirely inside my own head.

The corporate world loves finished things. Finished roadmaps. Finished strategies. Finished people.

Finished people are usually called "managers". Or at least have manager somwhere in their title.

What interests me now are unfinished systems. 

New languages, strange tools, half-formed ideas. Experiments that fail publicly and leave useful wreckage behind.

This site exists because I spent long enough inside organizations where appearing certain was often rewarded more than being curious.

Curiousity ages better.

So everything here remains in beta by design.

Some notes will contradict future notes.

Some projects will collaps under their own ambition.

Some ideas deserve exactly that fate.

The alternative is pretending that growth arrives fully documented and properly versioned.

It doesn't.

It arrives looking suspiciously like confusion.

The beta label stays because I have no interest in becoming another finished product.

The market is already oversupplied with those.
